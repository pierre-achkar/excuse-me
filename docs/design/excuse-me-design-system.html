EXCUSE ME

Design system v1

Visual and interaction reference for the excuse shop. Companion to M1.2, which defines the linguistic model this design serves. Every colour, type and layout decision below exists to support one thing: the user receives an atomic claim they will rephrase themselves, in a session short enough to feel like a visit rather than a task.

Flow

Four taps fill seven of M1.2's eight input fields, because a single situation choice resolves intent, action, context and obligation together.

   shop entry
       │
       ▼
   dialogue  four beats with the owner
       │
       │   1  what's the damage   →  intent · action · context · obligation
       │   2  when                →  timing
       │   3  who                 →  audience and relationship
       │   4  how loud            →  channel and tone
       ▼
   card reveal  atomic claim, plus a repair option when warranted
       │
       ▼
   exit  session ends

   collection  opened independently, any time

Why not eight beats

Asking each M1.2 dimension separately produces a form wearing a costume. Repair preference, the eighth field, is optional in the model and gets no beat — it appears on the card only for high-obligation situations or a repeat cancellation on the same person.

The two-layer rule

Pixel art lives in the shop and on the cards. Everything else stays clean.

Pixel layerShop scene and cards. Hard outlines, flat saturated fills, chunky borders, pixel typeface for names and stamps. Glitch treatment belongs here and nowhere else.

Clean layerCollection, card detail, settings, onboarding. Quiet, generous, no pixel typeface, no glitch. Shares only the violet accent.

Pixel art applied everywhere becomes wallpaper and stops signalling anything. Confining it marks the moment of magic and leaves the rest of the app legible.

Two consequences for build: pixel assets scale by integer multiples only, and the pixel typeface never carries more than three words.

Owner

Partly obscured by design — hood up, face in shadow, the glowing eyes and cigarette ember as the only readable features. Cool and mysterious, not threatening.

That line is easy to cross in execution: darker palettes and sharper silhouettes tip him into horror fast. Saturated colour and a relaxed offering posture are what keep him friendly.

Motion

Card reveal400ms, stepped easing — steps(6), so it reads as frames rather than a CSS transition. The one showpiece.
Chip press120ms scale-down. Happens four times a session, so it stays fast.
Dialogue change180ms crossfade. Sprite holds a two-frame idle at 600ms.
Glitch60–100ms chromatic bursts, every 8–20 seconds, unpredictable, shop scene only.
Clean layer200ms ease-out, or nothing at all.

Reduced-motion preference disables the glitch entirely and reduces the card reveal to a crossfade.

Safety carried into the UI

These follow from M1.2 and constrain the design directly, not as backlog items.

No dispatchNo send, share or copy as a primary action. The card is received, not sent. Nothing uses message-bubble styling, a recipient field, or messaging-app chrome.

Absurd stays separatedThe Absurd and dramatic family is visually distinct from the real-world families, so playful invention is never mistaken for a genuine emergency claim.

Repeats offer repairA second or third cancellation on the same person surfaces a repair direction, not a bigger excuse. Offered quietly and easy to skip — never styled as a scolding.

No fabricated evidenceNo fake screenshots, call logs or notifications. A hard product boundary.

Rarity never rewards escalationBecause rarity is decoupled from tone, Unhinged is not "better". If rarity tracked tone, collection pressure would push users toward socially riskier excuses.
